Shamal Mille: truing, tension check (Oct 27, 2026)

October 27, 2026

A customer brought in the front and rear wheels of a Shamal Mille for service.

They took them to a nearby shop in Tokyo, and when they requested truing work, they were told the nipples seemed seized and even though they had the proper tools, the nipples might twist the spokes, so they couldn't fix it and sent them back. That's why we're handling it here at the shop.

Apparently the front wheel in particular has variance in spoke tension.


I confirmed the customer hadn't touched them, but there were nipples starting to strip.



The provisional center is off. Beyond that, there's lateral runout, and before that, there's pronounced radial runout. It's radial runout so severe you can tell it just by holding the wheel and spinning it by hand, without even putting it on a truing stand.


At the phase where the radial runout toward the outer edge is greatest, the phase where the gauge only touches the rim as the wheel turns, I put a marker tape.


Next, I placed the gauge from the side to the very outer edge of the rim at that phase.



↑At other phases it looks like this. Even in the two images above, the rim's vertical position is different.


When I collected the nipples I judged needed replacement without being mindful of the radial runout issue, they turned out to be three consecutive rim holes right around the runout phase. Interesting.

By the way, the nipples weren't seized at all. I've written before that "seized-ness is subjective—a nipple I can't loosen gets called seized, and while I have examples of loosening someone else's seized-deemed nipples, I have zero examples of the reverse." But if I'm going to define things while saying "seized-ness is subjective," I think a good definition for seized-candidate status would be "when you loosen the nipple, white powder crumbles out from the wrench pocket." These nipples all loosened smoothly, and no powder came out at all.

Also, these aluminum spoke aluminum nipples with black anodize finish—black aluminum nipples clearly seize much less readily than silver ones, so when I first saw the black aluminum nipples, I predicted "probably (for me) not seized."

I mean, if we call this level of condition seized, most Shamal Ultra and Racing Zero wheels couldn't be serviced at all...


↑The replaced nipples


↑Flipped over, it looks like this. According to that nearby shop's road bike comment, there's variance in spoke tension, but that's totally obvious—if you do truing play and loosen the nipples at a particular phase enough to create pronounced radial runout, of course that spot will have lower tension!


Fixed it.



Radial and lateral runout corrected, center dead-on.


Usually I remove the marker tape after the work, but this time I left it so the customer could see it before I removed it. Masking tape doesn't leave residue.




～To the parent/owner～
Please note that there are absolutely no marks from wrench contact on the new nipples.


Next, the rear wheel. They said they thought there was no problem but sent it anyway, but there was one spot with radial runout.



The provisional center is dead-on.


Just one nipple was catastrophically stripped—of the three flats on the hex head grip, one was in a state where the tool completely slipped off.


But even so it wasn't seized, so without adding new damage to the grip flats


I got it out.



The provisional center after replacing the nipple and correcting radial and lateral runout. The rim was shifted toward the anti-freewheel side, so



I centered it.



Fixed. I'm not removing the marker tape on this one either. The replaced nipples—three from the front wheel, one from the rear—I'd usually throw away, but this time I'm sending them to the customer.
