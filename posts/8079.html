Fixed a Zonda rear wheel that apparently can't be repaired immediately in Nagano Prefecture

October 23, 2026

A customer left a Zonda rear wheel with me.

Today is Friday, but
on Tuesday they called to ask
whether this rear wheel could be fixed.
I told them that as long as the rim isn't bent,
I could almost certainly fix it
the same day they brought it in.

Since our shop is closed Wednesday and Thursday,
they said they'd bring it in directly on Friday,
but the customer
is actually from Nagano Prefecture
(they said I could mention this)
coming all this way just to have
the spokes on a Zonda replaced.

The reason being that there's a race
this coming Sunday,
and tomorrow Saturday is the test ride day,
so they absolutely had to get this wheel fixed
by then.
They found out about our shop from
a nearby bike shop
(with whom I have an agreement not to name)
and according to that shop,
there might not be anywhere in Nagano Prefecture
that keeps spare Zonda spokes in stock.
This is just one shop's opinion, of course.

I'm not particularly fond of
other shops casually recommending us
(we must not speak our name lightly),
but this time there's one thing I can appreciate:
they haven't unnecessarily fiddled with it
and have left it as-is.

There was a similar situation in the past
where I got absolutely furious (→here),
but that was about a Specialized store not being able to fix
a Roval wheel, so
the content—or rather my feelings about it—are completely different.


A portable bike stand got caught in the spinning rear wheel,
and one spoke on the non-freewheel side is broken,


and one spoke on the freewheel side
just before that in the rotation is bent.



All fixed.



A wheel with even one broken spoke won't show
a temporary center, so
after replacing the spoke
and doing the full lateral and radial truing,
I applied the centering gauge, and
as shown in the image above, it was guided to perfect center
just as it was originally dead center.
The bearing preload was actually on the tight side,
but the customer said they'd never touched it,
so it may have been that way from the start.
When replacing the non-freewheel side spoke,
I had to pull the hub axle out to some extent toward the right side,
so I also adjusted the bearing preload at that time.

As for the bent spoke,
when deformation is caused by an object wrapping around it just once,
the spoke is straight up to the point where it's bent.


↑Here's the break point of the fractured spoke.


The area near the threads was also deformed.


About the spare spokes.
The part number ZOC means Zonda Clincher.
In the case of Bola, if the initial model is Bola Tubular,
the model number BOT spokes
are sometimes shared with Clincher rim models.

Among the three-digit numbers, when the tens and ones places
are 16, those are front spokes,
18 is rear right, and 19 is rear left.
17 is skipped because this Zonda
is laced radially on both sides with the same spoke length,
and if the left and right lengths were different,
front right would be 16 and front left would be 17.

So the spare spokes for the first-generation C15 rim Zonda
are WH-016/018/019ZOC,
but these spokes have the hundreds digit advanced by one
because they're for the second-generation Zonda with C17 rim.
Only the first-generation Zonda has
the freewheel side spokes with a different spoke weight than the non-freewheel side,
but when the second-generation Zonda came out,
it was changed to equal-diameter spokes on both sides.

I have the bias
(with evidence from this case)
that Nagano Prefecture is an area where there are only shops
that can't immediately solve even this level of Zonda problem,
but since the customer came from Nagano Prefecture spending time and travel money,
I feel badly about it, so if there's a Nagano Prefecture shop
that could ultimately fix this Zonda to the same precision as ours or better
and is reading this, please step forward.
I'll mention you here.

I wrote "ultimately" because
what matters is that it gets fixed in time for Sunday's race
after I heard about it on Tuesday.
In other words, while the OEM spokes will eventually be ordered,
in the meantime you could make a temporary fix
with spokes of similar spoke weight
to get the wheel into a usable state.
In that case, once you know the spoke weight of Zonda spokes,
for example with CX-RAY and CX Sprint,
you need to know which one would be more appropriate to use.

Let's call repairing with OEM spokes "Solution A",
and getting the wheel into a usable state when A can't be done right away "Solution B".
When you step forward, please tell me whether it's A or B.
If it's A, I'll recommend you as-is,
but if it's B, I'll ask some technical questions.

If no one steps forward, unfortunately
my bias will not be corrected.
